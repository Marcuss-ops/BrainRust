The harbour authority of Bristol asked the engineer Hannah Poole in 2019 to review the renovation of the Harbour Stadium. The Harbour Stadium lies in Bristol and has been running since 1976. Daniel Frost, a technician with the maintenance crew, documented the pumps, the sensors, the net and the helmet before every shift. Northwater Limited estimated a saving of 36%, while Quay Power Limited projected 19%. In 2004 Poole was 19 years old and paid 11 pounds for a tour; those figures describe one personal visit and do not measure consumption. The plan fixed four steps: first replace the seals, second retest the pumps, third check the sensors after every rainfall, fourth publish the results at the end of the season. The inspection found no evidence of a pump defect, and the records did not demonstrate any annual saving. Frost logged the hour, the position and the volume collected in each trial for twelve weeks so the series stayed reproducible. Both companies used the same twelve month window but relied on different assumptions about the opening days of the stadium. The closing report keeps the favourable measurements beside the conflicting ones and never turns the companies' projections into achieved results. At the final meeting Poole presented the photographs of the replaced seals, the technical drawing of the pump, a map of the pipework and the camera used in the measuring room. Frost explained that one favourable week does not prove a lasting reduction and that comparable seasons are still missing. The minutes record which task was completed and which check stayed open, without turning the open question into a result. The city council published the file online so residents and clubs could check the numbers themselves.
