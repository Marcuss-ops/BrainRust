The school board of Norwich commissioned the architect Grace Whitfield in 2020 to supervise the renovation of the Central Stadium. The Central Stadium stands in Norwich and has served the county since 1961. Peter Doyle, the site manager, ordered scaffolding, ladders, cables and helmets before the roof work started. Studio Halden expected a saving of 28%, whereas Studio Mercer claimed 45%. In 1997 an annual pass cost 7 pounds; that value belongs to the fee schedule and says nothing about the building's energy demand. The order of works stayed fixed: seal the roof first, then replace the windows, afterwards bleed the radiators, finally return the shelves. A structural review established no damage to the heating system, and it demonstrated no lasting reduction in cost. Whitfield recorded temperature, humidity and ventilation times every week so that any deviation remained traceable. Both studios assumed the same service period but used different figures for the winter months. The report lists the unfavourable readings beside the favourable ones and does not present the studios' projections as verified outcomes. Doyle handed the staff a map of the roof sections, a camera for the documentation and the complete list of measuring points. The final note separates the measurement from the date of a repair and does not attribute every change to the heating system. A second visit in autumn confirmed the open items without improving the earlier estimates afterwards. The file stays available at the planning office so residents can follow the decision. Whitfield and Doyle compared the figures of both studios point by point and marked every assumption the text did not support. The report states which question is answered and which check has to be repeated next winter.
